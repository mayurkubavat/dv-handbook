### N4. `statlib` edge cases that remain (no example reaches any of them)

I measured these on a scratch copy:

- `first_n`'s cap of 2^40 makes `union_sum_draws([1e-13], 0.01)` return
  `None`, which its docstring reserves for a zero bin, although the answer
  is about 4.6e13 (`seeds_to_hit` gives 46051701859879). Fix: raise the
  cap, or document that `None` also means "beyond 2^40".
- `union_miss` computes `(1 - q) ** n`, which loses relative precision for
  small q. `union_sum_draws([1e-9, 0.5], 0.01)` gives 4605170314 where the
  exact answer is 4605170184. The error is negligible at the book's rates
  (q of at least 0.187 for every bin that is summed). Fix:
  `math.exp(n * math.log1p(-q))`.
- `pearson` on all-zero counts raises `ZeroDivisionError`, while `g_test`
  on the same input returns (0, 1, 1.0). `pearson` with every probability
  zero returns df = -1 and p = 1.0. `kl(p, q)` with q_i = 0 < p_i raises
  instead of returning inf. `ks_uniform` with a value of m or more raises
  `IndexError`. `quantile([])` raises `IndexError`.

These edge cases are correct: `quantile` with `None` (sorted last, all-None
gives `None`), `coupon_expect` with a zero bin (inf), `seeds_to_hit(0, d)`
(None), `union_split_draws` with a zero bin (None), the empty-sample
`ValueError`s, a count in a zero-probability cell (p = 0), an empty
zero-probability cell (ignored), `chao2` at t = 1 (S_obs), `cp_upper(0, 1)`
(0.95), and `cp_interval(0, 10)` (upper 0.3085).

### E2. Independent recomputation of the exact closure law

`seed_unit.py:174-183` computes P(all hit within n) =
sum_S (-1)^|S| (N_S/N)^n over the 11 bins that not every seed hits, which
is inclusion-exclusion under draws with replacement from the 1000 seeds. I
wrote a separate script, without `statlib` and without inclusion-exclusion,
that propagates the exact distribution over covered-bin states (2^11
states, transitions grouped by seed mask) seed by seed. It gives:

- P at n = 1, 2, 24, 25, 26 and 27: 0.060000, 0.235870, 0.987915,
  0.990119, 0.991925 and 0.993405. Median 5, q99 25.
- Largest difference from the `measured` column of `curve.tsv` over
  n = 1 to 60: 0.000049, which is the rounding to four decimals.
- At 25 seeds: P(some bin missed) 0.009881, P(two or more missed)
  0.002830, sum_i (1-p_i)^25 = 0.014920, and E[#missed] 0.014920. The last
  two agreeing is linearity of expectation, a cross-check on the
  `miss1` formula at `seed_unit.py:221-227`.
- 1 - sum_i (1-p_i)^n <= law(n) at every n from 1 to 60.
- With `fill_cg`'s climb added: median 9, q99 46.

All of these match `run.out:40-41,48-49` and the pins. The prose in the
chapter (the union bound "cannot be" above the measured curve, since a draw
misses bin i with probability exactly 1-p_i) is a correct statement about
this law. The other prose numbers also check out. The curve is at 0.55 at
5 seeds and 0.97 at 20, against 0.5486 and 0.9731 printed. The product is
0.14 at 5 seeds, against 0.1382. "Two seeds more" for a sum that is 1.5
times too large holds: ln 1.5 / -ln 0.813 = 1.95. The 22 bins with rates
above 0.8 (my count: 22) add nothing.

crash or a different assertion would now fail the check, not count as the
bug. `run_tway.sh:23` uses `grep ... >/dev/null` in place of `grep -q`, as
round 1 recommended.
