## Blocking findings

### B1. A witness cover is not evidence against a multi-cycle assumption on this tool, and block F's new cover shows it (soundness hole in the check the chapter relies on)

`examples/ch19-formal-techniques/cut-timer/cut.tcl:97-104` (block F, the
cover `c_rose` added in round 1), `chapters/ch19-formal-techniques.qmd:62`,
`:160`, `:759`.

Round 1 established, and the chapter now says at `:160`, that InFormal
judges a multi-cycle implication only once its whole window lies inside
the trace. The chapter draws the consequence for counterexamples only
("expect a spurious trace shorter than the window"). The same rule applies
to covers, and there it breaks the witness-cover check the chapter puts at
the head of every entry (`:62`) and at the center of its sign-off advice
(`:782`, `:815`): a cover is reported `covered` by a trace that violates an
assumption inside the trace, as long as the assumption's window has not
closed when the trace ends. So a vacuous multi-cycle assumption gives a
`proven` assertion *and* a `covered` witness cover of its antecedent.

Measured (scratch copy of block F; same design, same cut, `informal-kind`,
10 s; `a_resp {req && !busy |-> ##[1:3] gnt}` is false of the cut design):

```
assumption                                         a_no_early a_resp  c_rose        c_req2 (req && !busy ##2 1)
req && !busy |=> !expired [*2]       (block F)     proven     cex 4   covered, d 2  covered, d 3
req && !busy |=> !expired ##1 1'b0   (vacuous)     proven     PROVEN  covered, d 2  unreachable
req && !busy |=> !expired [*198] ##1 1'b0 (vac.)   cex 3      cex 4   covered, d 2  covered, d 3
```

With the vacuous two-cycle assumption, a false assertion is proven and
block F's shipped witness cover `c_rose` is still reached at depth 2. A
second probe on the bare handshake (no cut) gives the same picture:
`assume req && !busy |=> busy ##1 (busy && !busy)` (forbids every request)
makes `req && !busy |=> ##1 gnt` (refuted at depth 3 without the
assumption) `proven` by `informal-kind`, while `cover req && !busy` is
`covered` at depth 1; only `cover req && !busy ##2 1` is `unreachable`.
And directly: under `req && !busy |=> !expired [*N]`, the cover
`req && !busy ##1 u_timer.expired`, which every such assumption forbids,
is `undetermined` for N = 1 and `covered` at depth 2 for N = 2, 5 and 199.

What this does and does not break today: no other session of the chapter
has an assumption whose window exceeds one cycle (all others are `|->`,
single-cycle `|=>`, `$stable` or `s_eventually`; checked by grep over every
`assume` in the scripts and RTL), so no other shipped proof is affected,
and block F's `m_gap2` is not in fact vacuous (`c_req2` above is reached).
But the one witness cover round 1 added under a multi-cycle assumption is
exactly the case where it proves nothing, and the chapter's claim at `:62`
and `:759` ("the witness covers ... are the evidence that each proved
implication had something to say") is false for it. A reader who follows
the chapter's rule on this tool, with an assumption written as a window
(which the chapter itself shows in block F), gets a vacuous proof with a
green witness cover.

Fix (small):
1. In block F, extend the witness cover through the assumption's window:
   `cover -name c_win [format {req && !busy ##%d 1} $n]` with
   `expect covered c_win` (or `$rose(busy) ##[n-1] 1`). For n = 2 it is
   reached at depth 3 in well under a second; for n = 199 check the cost
   with `informal-bmc` (depth 200), or keep the extended cover only for
   the window of 2, which is the proven row that needs it.
2. At `:160`, after "the window of 199 ends long after the trace, so the
   trace survives", add one sentence: the same rule applies to covers, so
   on this tool a witness cover is evidence against an assumption only if
   the cover's trace runs to the end of that assumption's window; extend
   the cover by the longest window (`antecedent ##N 1`).
3. At `:759` (witness-cover entry) or its Pitfalls, one clause pointing at
   that sentence.

`param-width/width.tcl:27`, `pipe-256/pipe.tcl:61`,

the two limits it named (`mem-abs` D, `case-split` A). Four other
examples were not measured then. The tool's own report
(`build/informal/report.txt`, which keeps the seconds that `run.out`

To check rather than extrapolate, I ran all 24 examples on a copy whose

the first five rows above fail their `expect` (`contract`, `param-width`,
`pipe-256`, `two-word-ric3` exit `run=fail`, e.g. "unexpected: U1Full_C
is undetermined, expected covered"); the last three still pass. With
twice the printed limit (a machine twice as fast) all 24 pass and every
`run.out` is byte-identical to the committed one, so no `undetermined`
row is closer than a factor of two to a verdict (caveat: that run shared

Fix: raise the limits of the four sessions, keeping the undetermined
rows of the same sessions honest:

  undetermined row. Chapter `:462` needs no change.

- `ric3.tcl:29` 10 s to 30 s; `p_ahead` in the same run has no verdict

  "in ten" becomes "in thirty".
- `pipe.tcl:61` cannot simply be raised: `Full_C` in the same run,
  expected `undetermined`, is **covered at depth 513 in 31.8 s** by the
  same engine (measured with a 60 s limit), so a limit above about 15 s
  would flip it on a machine twice as fast. Either give `U1Full_C` its
  own run with 30 s and keep `Full_C` at 10 s, or (better, see N1) let
  `Full_C` run with 120 s, expect it `covered` at depth 513, and rewrite
  `:896` accordingly; the latter adds about 32 s to an example that
  takes 36 s now, still inside the 75 s budget.

## Non-blocking findings

### N1. `pipe-256`: the "out of reach" natural witness is reached in 32 s

`chapters/ch19-formal-techniques.qmd:896` ("bounded search gives no
verdict on it in ten seconds. The step teaches choosing witnesses a search
can reach") and the procedure at `:941`. `Full_C` (the pipe full, `P2`'s
antecedent) is covered by `informal-bmc` at depth 513 in 31.8 s with a
60 s limit, on the same boxed pipe under the same contracts. The text is
literally true for ten seconds, but presents a 32-second cover as out of
reach in the chapter's worked example. Either run it (see B2's fix,
which also removes the race on `U1Full_C`) and say the shallow witnesses
are a choice for budget, or keep the 10 s run and add "it is reached at
depth 513 in about half a minute on this machine".

### N2. `ag` C: a finite cover is no evidence for a liveness assumption

`examples/ch19-formal-techniques/ag/ag.tcl:32` (`C_out`, added in round
1). The chapter does not claim `C_out` witnesses `M_in` (`G_out` is not
an implication, so `:62` does not apply), but the cover cannot do that
job, and the fairness entry (`:743-747`) and assumption sanity entry
(`:765`) teach covers as the sanity check. Measured on `blk` with
`informal-ic3`, 5 s:

```
M_in                            G_out    G_false = s_eventually (out && !out)   C_out
s_eventually in                 proven   cex, depth 4                           covered, d 2
s_eventually (in && !in)        proven   PROVEN                                 covered, d 2
s_eventually (in && !rst_n)     proven   PROVEN                                 covered, d 2
```

With an unsatisfiable fairness assumption a false liveness assertion is
proven and `C_out` is still covered. The shipped `M_in` is fine
(`G_false` has a lasso). Suggest replacing or supplementing `C_out` with
`assert -name G_none {s_eventually 1'b0}` and `expect cex G_none` (a fair
lasso exists under the assumption), and one sentence in the fairness
entry: a fairness assumption is checked by refuting a deliberately false
liveness assertion under it, not by a cover.

### N3. `protocol` D proves an implication with no witness cover of its antecedent

`examples/ch19-formal-techniques/protocol/proto.tcl:27-31`: `a_in_held
{i_valid && !i_ready |=> i_valid}` is proven; the session's only cover,
`c_in_drop`, is (by design) unreachable. That contradicts the letter of
`:62` ("every implication proved in an example has a witness cover of its
antecedent") and the round-1 log's claim that the scan found none left.
The proof is not vacuous: `cover i_valid && !i_ready` is covered at depth
1 in the same setup. Add that cover with `expect covered` (it also makes
the session's point sharper: the antecedent happens, and the wrong-role
assumption forces the consequent).

### N4. `two-word-ric3` B: the witness cover is not the antecedent

`two-word-ric3/ric3.tcl:28`: `c_both {pop2 && out1}` stands for the
antecedents of `p_head1` and `p_head2`. It implies them only if the FIFO
is correct. The literal antecedents are cheap: `in1 && !out1 && a1 == '0`
is covered at depth 2 and `in2 && !out2 && a2 == '0` at depth 3
(`informal-bmc`, same assumptions). Suggest those two covers, so the
session matches `:62` exactly.

### N5. Witness covers that are reached trivially

`symmetry` `c_wait<i>` (depth 1, `req[i] && !gnt[i]`) and `symconst`
`c_slot` (depth 1) are correct antecedent covers and meet `:62`, but
they say nothing about the interesting part of the scenario (a wait of
several cycles; a push to slot `sa` that is later read). Round 1
measured the richer covers (a four-cycle wait of port 0 at depth 5 under
`m_hold0`). Optional.

and B-N6 (`assume -disable` on an RTL assumption) remain as the round-1
