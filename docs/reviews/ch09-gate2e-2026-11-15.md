## Blocking findings

### E1. L88 says the examples use only the two implications, and points at the wrong column
- L88: "Of everything in the table the chapter's examples use the two
  implications and nothing else, and the last column says why a reader
  should be careful with the rest on the book's simulator."
- The examples also use the table's first row, a fixed delay. `##1 1'b1`
  is in `handshake/hs_check.sv:29` (`Waited_C`), `fifo-checker/fifo_sva.sv:61`
  and `:63`, `axi-rules/axil_master_sva.sv:40,42,44` and
  `vacuity-formal/vacuity.tcl:32`. L52 lists "a delay of a fixed number of
  ticks" in the subset, the Pitfall at L228 prescribes `a ##1 1`, and L374
  explains the `##1 1'b1` covers. `goto-miss/tb_goto.sv:17` uses `[->2]`
  as well, on purpose.
- @tbl-ch09-seq has four columns. The last is "On the formal tool
  measured"; the simulator's verdicts are in the third.
- Fix: "Of everything in the table the chapter's checkers use the two
  implications and, in their covers, a fixed delay of one tick, and the
  third column says why a reader should be careful with the rest on the
  book's simulator."

### E2. L297 says every checker in the chapter reads an asynchronous reset in `disable iff`
- L297: "a checker that reads an asynchronous reset in its `disable iff`,
  as every checker in this chapter does, draws a lint warning ...; the
  examples switch that one warning off in their build flags".
- `vacuity/ack_check.sv` has no reset and no `disable iff` (its header:
  "The responder has no reset, so neither property names one").
  `bounded-response/resp_check.sv` is bound to a responder whose reset is
  synchronous (`responder.sv:14`, `always_ff @(posedge clk) if (!rst_n)`).
  In `handshake/tb_handshake.sv` `rst_n` is a wire off the tick counter.
  The waiver `-Wno-SYNCASYNCNET` is present only in `fifo-checker`,
  `axi-rules`, `fifo-formal`, `fifo-proof`, `assume-formal` and
  `axi-formal`.
- Fix: "as the FIFO and AXI checkers do" in place of "as every checker in
  this chapter does", and "those examples switch" for "the examples
  switch".

### E3. Appendix F, L255: a statement about `check-examples.sh` that the branch's change gives an exception to

## Non-blocking findings

N1. L331 and L349 call `expect` and `finish` "two procedures the book's
scripts share"; `examples/mk/informal.tcl:1`, printed at L351, says
"three procedures", and the third, `trace`, is used in the first script
the reader sees (`fifo.tcl:17`) and never mentioned. Add "and `trace`,
which names the waveform file of a counterexample".

N2. `Answer_A` (`ready |-> valid`, L131) is this example's own rule. AXI
lets READY be high with no VALID, and L468 relies on that freedom. One
clause at L131 ("a rule of this example's sink, not of AXI") would stop a
reader from carrying it to a real bus.

N3. L135: "On every tick but one they say the same thing." They also
differ on the first tick after any later reset: an attempt of the `|=>`
form that started under reset is disabled, and the `$past` form reads the
reset tick. "the first tick a tool checks, and the first after each
reset" is exact, and it is what the counterexample at L470 shows.

N4. L454: "each of the three was hit once, which is all a single run can
offer". The cover counts ticks, so a first offer that waits k ticks counts
k. "one offer" for "once" is what the next clause argues.

N5. L358: "There is no bound to set" is a claim about the tool with no
citation and no probe. `docs/fpv-shell.md` at 5114330 lists
`set_prove_time_limit` and no depth command; cite `[@informal-tool]`.

N6. Printed listing comments name "the first chapter" (`fifo.tcl:1`,
`rtl/fifo_fixed.sv:1`, not a label, since a comment cannot carry one). It
is an ordinal for the opening chapter and will not move; noted only
because the house rule is about numerals.
