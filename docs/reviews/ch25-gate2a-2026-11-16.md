## Verdict

**Needs another pass.** 4 blocking findings, 20 non-blocking.

The chapter is strong. The matrix as the one object every section explains,
the four-way classification of survivors, the miter whose comparison is
stated as the definition of "equivalent", the netlist population run in
full so that sampling can be checked against truth, and the two harness
Pitfalls are all correct and well evidenced. Every number in prose matches
its `run.out`, `measure.out` or table, and every interval reproduces. The
theory is right on the points the brief names: the two hypotheses and their
attribution (Jia and Harman; Budd et al. 1980 posing coupling as open),
strong/weak/firm mutation and their credits, the RIP conditions with
revealability via the 2019 survey, duplicates and subsumption, schemata,
TCE, the Verilator initial-value semantics, the `$value$plusargs`
first-match rule, the `parallel_case` assertion, MCY's bounded filter and
EQGAP, and the vendor labelling of every Certitude source. Fairness to
earlier chapters is good: the coverage testbench's zero is presented as the
correct result of a measurement Chapter 8 said it was making, and the
`Sync_A` paragraph says in so many words that the assertion is not wrong
and should not be deleted.

The blocking findings are all text fixes; no example needs to change. One
states a false principle about intervals of a ratio, the question the brief
singles out. One says the async-reset mutant is equivalent in every model
the chapter's tools build, four lines below a Verilator run that
distinguishes it. One splices a stretch of output that contradicts the
sentence introducing it. One is the worked example's model sentence, which
breaks the reporting rule the chapter laid down for white-box kills.

## Blocking findings

**B1. Lines 457 and 549 (and the draft log's answer to Exercise 3): the
stated reason a "score" above one appears is false, and the true reason is
not given.**

Line 457: "But at the upper end of the equivalent fraction's interval the
same division gives $0.8905/(1-0.126) = 1.019$, a "score" above one. The
ratio of two estimates is not bounded by the ratio of their intervals'
ends". Line 549: "The ratio of two estimates is not bounded by the ratio of
their intervals."

The score $k/(1-e)$ is increasing in $k$ and in $e$, so whenever the true
$k$ lies in $[k_L, k_U]$ and the true $e$ in $[e_L, e_U]$, the true score
*is* bounded by $k_L/(1-e_L)$ and $k_U/(1-e_U)$. Endpoint substitution
gives a valid (conservative, joint confidence at least 90% for two
independent 95% intervals) range; it is not "unbounded". What goes wrong is
different and more instructive: the interval for $e$ was computed without
the constraint that an equivalent mutant can never be killed, so
$k \le 1 - e$ always. Here $k$ is the population rate, known exactly, so
$e \le 1 - 0.8905 = 0.1095$; the interval's upper end 0.126 is a value the
population cannot have, and the division turns that impossibility into a
"score" of 1.019. The general lesson is that two separately computed
intervals ignore the relation between the quantities, so combining them
gives a range that is wide, of lower joint confidence than either, and can
include impossible values; the joint sample is the fix because it
estimates the score directly. A reader who takes away the sentence as
written will distrust a correct bound and miss the real defect.

Fix: replace the sentence at 457 with something like "The range is not
wrong so much as careless: the interval for $e$ was computed alone, and it
reaches values that this kill rate rules out, since an equivalent mutant
is never killed and so $e$ can be at most $1 - 0.8905 = 0.1095$. Two
intervals computed separately know nothing of the relation between their
quantities; combining their ends gives a range of lower joint confidence
than either, wide enough to hold impossible values. An interval for a
derived quantity has to be derived, or the quantity estimated directly, as
the joint sample does." Change the summary bullet at 549 to match ("Plugging
interval ends into a ratio ignores the relation between the two quantities
and can give a score above one"), and the draft log's Exercise 3 answer to
"it shows that 0.126 is incompatible with the kill rate, since
$e \le 1-k = 0.1095$".

**B2. Line 253: "The designs are not equivalent in silicon, and they are
equivalent in every model the chapter's other tools build." The Verilator
run spliced four lines above distinguishes them.**

`equiv-reset/run.out` lines 9-10 show Verilator, one of the chapter's
tools, giving `empty=1` for the repaired FIFO and `empty=0` for `m05` after
a reset pulse between edges. The simulator models the asynchronous edge
faithfully; what hides `m05` is the book's testbenches' stimulus (reset held
across a rising edge, line 245), not the simulator's model. Only the formal
tool's model makes the two the same machine (Chapter 9, line 357: "Reset is
one cycle, and it is synchronous ... The tool models every reset that
way"). As written, the sentence teaches the opposite of the example: that
simulation, like formal, cannot see the difference.

The same paragraph is also where the chapter should say what the proof
does and does not establish, which the brief asks for: the IC3 proof shows
equivalence on every input sequence *in which reset acts only at clock
edges*, starting from the tool's one-cycle reset; it says nothing about
outputs while reset is held between edges or about a reset that rises and
falls between edges. That qualifier applies to every "equivalent" verdict
in the chapter, not only `m05`'s (for the others it is harmless because
both copies share the same reset structure), so the Definition at line
212-214 should name the tool's reset model too.

Fix: "The designs are not equivalent in silicon, and the simulator can tell
them apart, as the run shows; they are the same machine only to the formal
tool, and indistinguishable to every book testbench because each changes
reset only across a clock edge." Add to the Definition at 212: "both
started from the tool's one-cycle reset, under its model, in which every
reset acts at a clock edge (@sec-ch09-...)".

**B3. Lines 459-462: the splice of `netlist-formal/measure.out` lines 83-94
does not show what the sentence says.**

The text: "The fourteen non-equivalent mutations that the property set
misses are not gaps in the specification. The miter separates them only at
depth 16 to 31:". The spliced rows are 10 of the 14 misses (7648, 7691,
7759, 7832, 7910, 8096, 8105, 8108, 8130, 8394) plus two rows the property
set *kills* at depth 15 (`row 8229 a_integrity@15 differs@15`, `row 8270
a_integrity@15 differs@15`). Four misses (1815, 3453, 5944, 7033, at depths
16, 17, 16, 16) are not shown. A reader checking the listing against the
sentence finds two rows that contradict it and cannot count fourteen.

The data themselves are a better lesson than the text draws: in this file
every `a_integrity` kill is at depth 15 or less and every miss at 16 or
more, which is what a three-second bound looks like.

Fix: either have the default run print the fourteen misses as their own
block (preferred: one line each, mutation, mode, line, miter depth, and the
random test's verdict, which is "killed" for all fourteen per `pop.tsv`)
and splice that; or keep the stretch and say what it is: "A stretch of the
output, in mutation order; a dash is a miss. The property set's kills stop
at depth 15 and its misses start at 16, which is the reach of three seconds
of bounded search:". Either way the count of fourteen should be visible or
stated as the default run's check.

**B4. Line 523 (Step 5, the sentence): the model report counts `m44`'s
white-box kill in "37 of the 44" and "40 of 44", against the rule the
chapter set at line 266.**

Line 266: "The remedy is to report kills by white-box assertions separately

Fix: "kills 37 of the 44 source mutants ... at default initial values, 36

of the 39 non-equivalent mutants at the ports, a score of 39 of 39". Check
the 36/39 arithmetic against `fifth-tb/run.out` before printing it (the
default survivors are `m05 m09 m11 m13 m32 m36 m43`, so non-equivalent kills
at default are 39 - 3 = 36; randomized survivors are the four equivalents).

## Non-blocking findings

### Technical and numbers

**N1. Lines 30 and 32: "identical code coverage" and "99% or more of the
toggles" overstate.** The random test toggles 329/330 (99.7%), the coverage
testbench 326/330 (98.8%), per `opening/run.out` lines 21-23. "Three of them
reach ... 99% or more of the toggles" is false for the coverage testbench,
and "Two testbenches with identical code coverage" is false for toggles. The
point survives intact. Fix: "the same line, branch and expression coverage,
and toggle coverage within a point of each other"; "98.8% or more".

**N2. Line 376: "The modes themselves matter less than the population's
composition; the random test's kill rate by mode ... varies by a few
points".** The spliced table (lines 69-75) shows the directed test ranging
from 0.166 (`cnot1`) to 0.426 (`inv`), a factor of 2.6. The claim holds
for the random test only, which is the testbench least sensitive to
anything. Fix: state both: "the random test's rate varies by a few points
by mode, the directed test's by a factor of two and a half: for a weak
testbench the mode mix matters too."

**N3. Line 321: "each property earns its place by a set of mutants that
only it, or it and one other, can kill".** Computed from
`property-set/measure.out`: `FullBackward_A` alone kills `m39`-`m41`,
`Sync_A` alone `m44`, `a_integrity` alone `m15`, `m16`, `m33`;
`EmptyPtrs_A`, `EmptyExact_A` and `NoOverflow_A` kill something with one
other; `FullForward_A`, `PushFull_A` and `PopEmpty_A` kill nothing that two
or more others do not also kill. Fix: "most properties earn their place
...; three (`FullForward_A`, `PushFull_A`, `PopEmpty_A`) kill nothing the
others miss on this population, which says their mutants are not in this
operator set, not that they are useless". That is a useful observation in
its own right.

**N4. Line 545 (summary) and objective 5: "The FIFO's five equivalent
mutants are of three kinds".** `m44` is the fifth, and the survivors table
(`tbl-ch25-survivors`, class "equivalent at the ports; killed by Sync_A")
and the matrix figure ("at the ports only") give it a class of its own,
while the summary lists three kinds that do not include it. Say where `m44`
belongs: its equivalence needs a relational invariant between the copies
(the mutant's count is minus the original's, modulo 32), so it is
"under a reachable-state invariant", with the white-box kill as a separate
remark. Then use the same name in the table, the figure band and the
summary.

**N5. Lines 218, 229, objective 4 and Exercise 6: "bounded search cannot
decide them however long it runs: it can only ever refute".** Chapter 18
teaches the completeness threshold (@sec-ch18, line 110-113, and line 539:
"It proves nothing beyond the bound it reached unless that bound is a
completeness threshold"). For this miter the state is finite and its
deepest reachable state is not very deep (free initial memory, pointers
reachable in at most sixteen steps), so a bounded search that knew a
threshold would decide equivalence. The tool does not know one, which is
the real limit. Fix: "a bounded search that does not know a completeness
threshold (@sec-ch18-...) can only refute"; in Exercise 6, "Why can this
tool's bounded search never report ...".

**N6. Lines 339, 381 and 440: who does the weighted sampling, and what
"even sampling" means.** Line 381 lists, among the four things "MCY ...
adds", that it "draws its list as a weighted sample", citing `mcy-docs`.
Line 440 then attributes the weighting to `mutate -list N` itself ("as
@sec-ch25-netlist said, it chooses candidates with weights"), and line 339
quotes the help as "using an even sampling" without saying how an "even"
sampling can be non-uniform. The research note (3.6) says the weighting is
`mutate`'s (eight candidate lists, weights, a source-coverage heuristic),
documented on MCY's `mutate.html` page. Fix: at 381, "It asks `mutate` for
fewer mutations than exist, so its list is `mutate`'s weighted sample: ...";
at 339, one clause: "even across wires, cells and source locations, which
is not uniform over mutations".

**N7. Line 436: "The interval holds the truth at least 94.5% of the time
... as a conservative interval should."** 0.945 is below the nominal 0.95,
so the sentence reads as self-contradictory. With 2,000 samples the
Monte Carlo standard error of a coverage near 0.95 is about 0.005, so 0.945
is within noise. Say so ("the lowest, 0.945, is within the 0.005 standard
error of 2,000 draws"). Related, line 440: the draw is without replacement
from 9,281 (n/N = 10.8% at 1,000), so the count is hypergeometric, not
binomial; the binomial interval is then conservative, which is worth one
clause under objective 8 ("what the binomial model assumes about how the
sample was drawn").

**N8. Line 229, line 309 and `mutlib.py` lines 5-11: three words for the
front end's refusal of `m06`/`m07`.** Line 229 calls them "kills by the
tool, like the lint kills"; line 309 says "a refusal is not a
counterexample"; the harness header calls a design that does not compile
"stillborn (left out of every score)". Verilator compiles both, so they are
not stillborn for the simulation scores, but the reader is given three
framings. Pick one ("refused by the formal front end and by Yosys; a
synthesis flow would reject them, which is a kill by the flow, counted
apart") and use it in both places.

**N9. Line 290: the Pitfall prescribes a control the chapter never shows for
its own comparison.** "check the comparison by running the miter on the
original against itself, where it must not find a difference". The only
self-miter run (`miter-differ/run.out` line 53) is the every-cycle
comparison, which is supposed to fail. The guarded comparison is never run

another route: `m32`'s netlist is identical to the original's
(`source-mutants/run.out` line 75) and rIC3 proves its miter, so the `m32`
proof is the control. Say so in one sentence, or add the run.

**N10. Line 263: "The example `white-box` shows both halves and the count

**N11. Lines 164-169: "that line already says why".** The spliced line is

**N12. Line 199: "The tool is the miter of @sec-ch17".** Chapter 17 and

the glossary entry with one sentence for the sequential case, pointing to
@sec-ch25-equiv.
