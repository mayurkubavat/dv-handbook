## Blocking finding

**E-B1. Line 975 (Summary), and in the same words line 607 (caption of
@fig-ch19-ag) and line 991 (Exercise 9): the assume-guarantee rule for
safety is stated with the register condition alone, which the round-2
fix and block E of `ag/` show is not enough.**

Line 975: "Assume-guarantee is sound for safety when a register breaks
every loop, and the flat ring agreed".

Round 2 (C-B1) was blocking because the chapter treated "every loop
passes through a register" as sufficient. The fix added the second
condition to the entry (line 595: "when every loop of mutual dependence
passes through a register and each block is proved on its own, with its
neighbor removed, so that the assumption reaches only that block's
inputs and nothing is assumed about its reset state"), and the new block
E of `ag/` measures what happens without it: on the ring itself, both
resets at 1, `x == 0` with `y == 0` assumed and `y == 0` with `x == 0`
assumed are both `proven` (`ag/run.out` lines 36-47) although both are
false, and the loop passes through a register in each direction. Line
605 calls block E "the warning that each block must be proved alone".
The Summary, the part of the chapter a reader returns to, still gives
the old one-condition rule as sufficient; a reader who applies it to the
ring of block E gets two false lemmas proven, the defect C-B1 removed
from the body.

The caption at line 607 ("the register in each block means a guarantee
at cycle t + 1 needs the other's only up to cycle t, so the circular
argument is sound") describes the example, whose blocks were proved
alone, so it is not false of the figure; but it names the register as
the whole reason, and readers take captions as rules. Exercise 9
("which registers make the safety ring's circular argument sound")
invites the same one-condition answer. These two are part of the same
fix, not separate findings.

Fix.
- Line 975: "Assume-guarantee is sound for safety when a register breaks
  every loop and each block is proved alone, with only its inputs
  assumed, and the flat ring agreed; proved on the ring itself, two
  false lemmas were each proven with the other assumed."
- Line 607, caption: "... needs the other's only up to cycle t, and each
  block is proved alone, so the circular argument is sound, and the
  flat proof agrees."
- Line 991: "In `ag`, which registers, and which way of running the
  proofs, make the safety ring's circular argument sound? Why do blocks
  A and E disagree about a block reset to 1, and why does the same
  argument not prove the liveness ring?"

## Teaching

The timer entry now teaches a portable lesson with its evidence: a tool
may judge a multi-cycle assumption late, the counterexample and the
witness cover both feel it, and the cure is a one-cycle gap or a cover
through the window. The vacuous-window run (lines 162-167) is the
clearest demonstration in the book that a green witness cover can be
the wrong witness. Block E of `ag/` turns the round-2 soundness fix
into something the reader can run. The worked example's step 1 now
explains why shallow witnesses come first and where the deep one runs,
which is better teaching than "out of reach". The one place where the
chapter would still mislead is its Summary bullet on assume-guarantee
(E-B1).
