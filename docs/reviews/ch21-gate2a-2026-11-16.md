## Verdict

**Needs another pass.** 2 blocking findings, 17 non-blocking.

The mathematics is sound almost everywhere. I re-derived and found correct
the geometric one-bin law and its log approximation (always high, since
$-\ln(1-p) > p$); $kH_k$; the Erdős-Rényi limit; the Poissonization argument
for $E[T]$ with unequal probabilities, including the Wald step; the
inclusion-exclusion law; both forms of the union bound and the claim that the
split form is never smaller; the zero-failure bound, the rule of three and
the Jeffreys ratio (its limit is $\chi^2_{1,0.95}/2 \big/ 3 = 0.640$); the
Clopper-Pearson definition; Pearson's statistic, the KS limit law and the
note that it is conservative for a discrete law; the bit-test sample-size
formula (113, 1047 and 26,265 all reproduce); the expansion $G \approx X^2$;
$G = 2nD(\hat p\|q)$; the CAN(2,k,2) formula and the construction argument
(sets of size $\lceil N/2\rceil$ in $N-1$ rows are pairwise incomparable and
pairwise intersecting); the pair and triple lower bounds of 24, 20 and 60;
the 200 legal rows and the 13 and 57 zero-probability tuples behind the
0.887 and 0.828 plateaus; linearity for expected t-way coverage; Good-Turing's
leave-one-out argument and the Markov-inequality step that makes $Q_1/T$ an
upper estimate of the chance of any new bin; Chao2 in both branches and the
incidence extrapolation; mutual information. Every number quoted in prose
matches its `run.out` or table, apart from the wording points listed below.
The seed-versus-cycle lesson is the strongest part of the chapter.

The other states a threshold for the clean-seed Pitfall that is about four
times too large. Both are text fixes; no example needs to change.

## Blocking findings

**B1. Line 200: the explanation of why the union bound is nearly tight, and
why the product joins it at the tail, is wrong, and the data contradict it.**

The text says: "On this covergroup it is barely so, 27 against 26, because the
rare bins have nearly equal rates and their miss probabilities add up as the
bound assumes." It goes on: "The four `*_x_full` cells, `full` and `almost`
are hit together or not at all: a seed that fills the FIFO usually hits all
six ... At the tail the two agree, because by then the question is whether
the last seed that fills the FIFO has come, and that is one event however many
bins hang on it."

What is wrong:

1. *Equal rates have nothing to do with how tight the bound is.* The gap
   between $\sum_i P(A_i)$ and $P(\bigcup_i A_i)$ is set by the joint misses,
   $P(A_i \cap A_j)$ and higher terms. The bound is tight when the miss events
   are nearly disjoint, whatever the rates. "Add up as the bound assumes" also
   misstates the bound: it assumes nothing. It is tight when overlaps are
   rare. If the six bins really were "hit together or not at all", their miss
   events would coincide, and the sum would over-count the risk about sixfold.
   That is the case in which the bound is loosest, which is the opposite of
   what the sentence uses it to explain.
2. *"One event however many bins hang on it" contradicts the chapter's own
   numbers.* If the tail were set by whether any seed has filled the FIFO,
   99% would need what `full` alone needs: 15 seeds (`seed-unit/run.out`
   line 47). The measured figure is 26, and `push_x_full` alone needs 23
   (line 46). The tail is set by the individual `op_x_full` cells being missed
   *in seeds that did fill the FIFO*. From `counts.tsv`, at n = 26 the misses
   are: `push_x_full` 0.0046, `idle_x_full` 0.0042, `pop_x_full` 0.0017,
   `both_x_full` 0.0009, and `full`/`almost` only 0.0003 each.
3. *"Usually hits all six" overstates the data.* Of the 271 seeds that show
   `full`, 137 (0.51) hit all four `op_x_full` cells. Each cell given `full`
   is hit at 0.69 to 0.87. If the cells were independent given `full`, the
   chance of all four would be 0.34, so they are positively correlated, but
   "hit together or not at all" is not true.
4. *Why the product equals the union bound at the tail is arithmetic, not
   design.* $1 - \prod_i(1-a_i) = \sum_i a_i - \sum_{i<j} a_i a_j + \cdots$,
   so when the $a_i = (1-p_i)^n$ are small, the independent-bins product and
   the union bound coincide for any rates. They agree at 27 because both
   ignore the joint misses. The resampled curve is lower (26) because joint
   misses do occur. A bootstrap of the 1000 seeds at n = 26 gives
   P(some missed) ≈ 0.008 against a sum of 0.0119, with P(two or more
   missed) ≈ 0.002.
5. Also on line 200, "the union bound is never above the measured curve" does
   not hold for `curve.tsv`. At n = 32 the guarantee is 0.9968 and the
   resampled value is 0.9965. This is within the resampling error of 2000
   orders (about ±0.0013), but the sentence is absolute.

Proposed fix: rewrite the three reasons.

- Tightness: "On this covergroup the bound is barely pessimistic, 27 against
  26, because at the tail the miss events are nearly disjoint: a run of 26
  seeds that misses one rare cell almost never misses a second, so the sum
  counts almost nothing twice."
- Small $n$: keep the correlation point with the measured strength: "a seed
  that fills the FIFO always hits `full` and `almost` and hits all four
  `op_x_full` cells about half the time, against a third if they were
  independent."
- Tail: "At the tail the product and the bound agree for an arithmetic
  reason: when every miss probability is small, $1-\prod(1-a_i)$ and
  $\sum a_i$ differ only by the products of pairs. What sets the tail is the
  individual full-FIFO cells, each missed in some seeds that do fill the
  FIFO, which is why 26 seeds are needed where reaching `full` alone needs
  15."
- Replace "never above" with "never above it beyond the resampling error".

**B2. Line 278: the threshold in "Anywhere to the left of that, a clean
regression of that generator is the expected outcome whether the bug is there
or not" is about four times too large.**

With $p = 8.45\times10^{-5}$, a run of $n$ seeds is clean with probability
$(1-p)^n \approx e^{-np}$. That probability is above one half only for
$n < \ln 2/p \approx 8{,}200$. At 10,000 seeds it is 0.43, and at the
35,000-seed crossing the sentence names it is 0.05. The crossing of $3/n$
with the true $p$ is where a clean run becomes a 5% event, the definition of
the 95% bound. It is not where a clean run stops being expected. As written,
the sentence teaches the reader the wrong reading of the bound, in the
figure the chapter calls "the Pitfall in one picture".

Proposed fix: "The bound falls as $3/n$, and it reaches the true rate of the
30-cycle generator only near 35,000 seeds, where a clean run of that
generator finally becomes a 5% event. Below about 8,000 seeds ($\ln 2/p$), a
clean run is more likely than not, whether the bug is there or not." The
8,200 is arithmetic on the printed 8.45e-05. If the house rule wants it on a
`run.out` line, add one line to `clean-seeds` that prints $\ln 2/p$.

## Non-blocking findings

### Technical precision

**N1. Line 175: "simulation agrees with it within its standard error at every
size".** For k = 8 the simulated mean is 22.0 against $8H_8 = 21.74$, with a
standard error of 0.14 (it prints as 0.1), so about 1.9 standard errors. For
k = 64 the gap is 1.8 against a standard error of 1.3. The example itself
checks "within 4 se" (`coupon.py` line 57). Fix: "within two standard
errors".

**N2. Line 112: "for the rare ones by a factor of two or more".** The
`seed-unit/run.out` ratios for the four `op_x_full` cells are 2.19, 2.36, 1.98
and 1.88. Fix: "by a factor of about two, and for `full` and `almost` more
than three".

**N3. Lines 398 and 411: the constraint claim goes past the evidence.** Line
398 says "the constraints make it worse". Line 411 says "The DMA's 3.9 to 8.7
is that last clause in numbers", that is, Arcuri and Briand's "arbitrarily
worse when constraints are present". The example has no unconstrained
baseline for the same space. The factor-by-factor ratio, 3.9, equals Kuhn,
Kacker and Lei's unconstrained 2-way average. What the example does show is
that the ratio depends on how the generator draws: uniform over legal rows
8.7, factor by factor 3.9. Fix: "Under constraints the ratio depends on how
the random tests are drawn, 3.9 to 8.7 on this space, which is the direction
of Arcuri and Briand's last clause". Alternatively, add an unconstrained run
of the same factors to `t-way`.

**N4. Line 443: "the estimate of the count is low until the sample has seen
most of the tail. That is what a lower bound looks like in practice."** The
single run on the spliced lines gives Chao1 = 213.3 at 800 draws, above the
truth of 200 (`unseen/run.out` line 6). Chao1 is a lower bound *in
expectation*, as line 430 says correctly. Fix: "on average the estimate is
low ...; a single run can overshoot, as the 800-draw row does".

**N5. Lines 477-482 and `info/run.out` lines 3-7: KL units.** The chapter
defines $D(p\|q)$ with the natural logarithm. The spliced "KL to uniform"
column is in bits (0.0708 for `:=`; in nats it is 0.0491). The very next
spliced block (lines 9-11) prints nats and labels them. A reader who checks
the first block against the definition gets a different number. Fix: label
the column "KL to uniform, bits" in `info.py`, or print it in nats.

**N6. Line 497: "would spend most of its cycles in the upper half of the
FIFO".** With the coverpoint's reachable bins `empty` {0}, `low` {1:7},
`high` {8:14} and `almost` {15} (`tb_fifo_cov.sv` lines 17-21), a uniform law
over the bins puts exactly half its cycles in 8 to 15. The more telling fact
is that it puts a quarter of them at exactly 15 entries. Fix: "would spend a
quarter of its cycles at fifteen entries".

**N7. Line 561: "the union bound said in advance that it would be with
probability at least 0.99".** That holds only if the pilot's rates are the
true rates. Line 554 says so; this sentence drops it. Fix: add "if the
pilot's rates are right".

**N8. Line 340: "would not survive a hundred".** The power at n = 100 for
q = 0.65 is 0.875 (`uniformity/run.out` line 36), so about one such bit in
eight survives 100 draws. Fix: "would be caught nine times in ten by about
113 draws".

**N9. Lines 455-457 and 135: "sees nothing unseen" against "adds a third of a
bin".** These are consistent in fact (26.0 against 25.7), but line 130's
"sees nothing unseen" is stated without qualification. Fix: "sees almost
nothing unseen".

## Teaching

A DV engineer who has read the constrained-random and coverage chapters can
follow this one. Each formula is stated with its conditions, checked against
a Python simulation of its own assumptions, and then set against a Verilator
run where an assumption breaks. That structure works.

The seed-versus-cycle lesson is clear and well evidenced: the 0.898 against
0.271 table, the 11-against-26 sizing error, the first-trigger hazard, and
the streak check on seed independence. The assumptions are stated where they
break: an unreachable bin, a generator that never reaches the bug, adaptive
generators, cycle-level counting, and pilot sampling error.

The density is high. Sections such as the clean-seed and uniformity sections
put three or four Definitions and four spliced blocks before the reader
reaches the point. That is a style choice, not a defect.

After B1 is fixed, the coupon section will teach correctly what makes a union
bound tight. That is worth getting right, because the In Practice recipe
tells the reader to size regressions with that bound.
