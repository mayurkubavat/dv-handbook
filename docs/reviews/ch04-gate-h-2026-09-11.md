## Serious (not blocking)

**H-S1 — `:104` puts quotation marks around a guideline the source words
differently, with no citation on the line.**

`chapters/ch04-simulation-fundamentals.qmd:104`, printed at folio 125:

> One guideline in the list, "do not mix blocking and non-blocking
> assignments in one block", is a corollary of the first two rather than
> something a region implies

The guideline, in the paper the list comes from (`nba.txt:194` and `:846`,
fetched from the archive capture the entry names):

```
Guideline #5: Do not mix blocking and nonblocking assignments in the same always block.
```

"in one block" is not "in the same always block", and "non-blocking" is not
"nonblocking". The words inside the marks are the chapter's, not the
source's. This is recorded as serious rather than blocking for two reasons:
the sentence does not attribute the words to anyone and carries no citation
(the list's citation is at `:93`), so it reads as the chapter naming a rule
rather than quoting one, and round G read this line (§3.2, row 104) and did
not treat it as a quotation. But the book's convention is that quotation
marks with a source in view mean the source's words, and the last blocker
in this chapter was one word inside quotation marks. The fix is either to
remove the marks — *One guideline in the list, that blocking and
non-blocking assignments are not mixed in one block, is a corollary…* — or
to quote exactly and cite on the line: *"Do not mix blocking and nonblocking
assignments in the same always block" [@cummings2000nba]*.

## Minor

**H-M1 — `:189` attributes a three-author paper's words to one author.**
*"Sutherland reads the standard as permitting exactly this: in his words, "a
compiler might, or might not, apply a default time unit" … [@sutherland2007gotchas]"*.
The paper's cover (`g2007.txt:1–15`) names Sutherland, Mills and Spear, and
the printed citation reads "(Sutherland et al. 2007)", so "his words" sits
beside a citation that says otherwise. `:149` does the same for the 2013
paper and is right there, because that paper is single-authored. Fix: "The
paper reads the standard as permitting exactly this: in its words, …" or
"Sutherland, Mills and Spear read the standard as…".

**H-M2 — `:231` "which is a defect in neither" is the chapter's gloss, not
the source's.** *"The sequel adds a fourth, ambiguity in the standard's own
text, which is a defect in neither [@sutherland2007gotchas]."* The sequel's
bullet (`g2007.txt:135–140`) says two kinds of ambiguity "occasionally occur
in these complex documents" and "can lead to differences in tool behavior";
its previous bullet (`:127–131`) says of tool differences "This is not a
problem with the definition of the … languages; it is a problem with software
tools." The paper assigns the first to the tools and the second to the
documents; it does not say an ambiguity is nobody's defect, and an ambiguity
in the standard's text is, on the paper's own account, a defect in the
document. Fix: "…ambiguity in the standard's own text, which the paper lays
at the document's door rather than the tool's" — or simply end the sentence
after "text".

**H-M3 — `racy.sv` starts with a two-line orphan at the foot of folio 132
(physical 136).** The image `hi-136.png` shows the Worked Example box, then
a listing box containing only `// racy.sv -- one variable, two processes,
one clock edge.` and `//`, then the folio; the remaining 22 lines are on
folio 133. This is the filter's stated choice for source listings ("left to
break"), and no house rule is broken, but a two-line head is the worst
possible break. Fix: give source listings a small request that cannot cost a
visible foot — `\Needspace{5\baselineskip}` for every included block that is
not `.text` — so a listing never begins with fewer than about four lines on
a page.

*Observations, not findings.* (i) `:80` "it is VHDL's term" carries no
citation. It is not a number and no house rule reaches it, and rounds C–G
let it stand; a reader who wants it sourced would need the VHDL standard,
which the book does not quote. (ii) `:200` still refers to "This book's own
plan", a document the reader cannot see; round G noted it and it breaks no
rule. (iii) Folio 136's 10% foot (table above) is a heading-at-page-foot
break, not an include-filter effect.
