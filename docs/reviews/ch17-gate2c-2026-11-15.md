## Blocking findings

### B1. Line 129: which paper holds which procedure is claimed from two unread papers

"That is the procedure of Davis, Logemann and Loveland
[@davis1962machine], built on an earlier procedure of Davis and Putnam
[@davis1960computing] and called DPLL after the four of them."

Both entries carry the note "Metadata from the publisher's Crossref
record; text not consulted", and research note 2.2 marks both `[V-CR]`
(metadata only). The sentence makes two content claims: that guess,
propagate and backtrack is the 1962 paper's procedure, and that it was
built on the 1960 one. Neither is recorded as read anywhere in the
project. (Both are, to my knowledge, true. That is not the standard the
chapter set for itself in round 1, where the same pattern for Haken was
B3.)

What a read source does support: the preprint of the Handbook's
proof-complexity chapter, already cited as `buss2021proof`, fetched again
today, says in its subsection on DPLL (1.3.1.1 of the preprint) that DPLL
"is named after the four collective authors, Davis, Putnam, Logemann and
Loveland," of "two of the primary sources for resolution and automated
solvers, namely [DP60] and [DLL62]", and describes it as a depth-first
search for satisfying assignments that tries a decision literal "first
true and then false". It does not say which paper contributed what.

Fix (one sentence, no new reading): "The procedure is called DPLL after
Davis, Putnam, Logemann and Loveland, the authors of the two papers it is
traced to [@davis1960computing; @davis1962machine], which is how the
*Handbook of Satisfiability* explains the name [@buss2021proof]." Add "the
subsection on DPLL (1.3.1.1 there)" to the `buss2021proof` note.
Alternatively read the two papers and say so in their notes; then the
present sentence can stand.

### B2. Line 335: what symbolic model checking does is claimed from a paper the records mark unread

"BDDs are the engine of symbolic model checking, which holds sets of
states as diagrams and does not list them [@burch1992symbolic]".

The entry's note is "Metadata from the publisher's Crossref record; text
not consulted". Research note 2.4 records only that the abstract of the
1990 conference version was "read in paraphrase", and the drafting entry
in `STATUS.md` says "Haken 1985 and Burch et al. unread, so nothing is
claimed from them". The sentence claims the paper's method (sets of
states held as diagrams, not enumerated), which is more than its title.
Round 1 fixed the Haken half of that `STATUS.md` sentence and left this
half.

Fix, either of: (a) cite the claim to the paper that was read. The
bounded-model-checking paper (`biere1999symbolic`, "Paper read") opens by
saying that BDDs have traditionally been the symbolic representation in
symbolic model checking; re-read its first two sentences and write "BDDs
are the traditional representation in symbolic model checking, as the
paper that introduced the method of this chapter's worked example says at
its start [@biere1999symbolic]; the method is that of Burch and others
[@burch1992symbolic]". (b) Read the abstract of the 1992 article on the
publisher's page and change the note to "Abstract read"; the abstract, as
I know it, supports the sentence as written.

## Non-blocking findings

**N1. Line 199: "a subclass that changes one method".** The listing
below it defines three: `__init__`, `analyze` and `pick`. The point
survives (only the decision rule differs; `analyze` returns what the
parent returns). Say "a subclass that changes how the next variable is
picked and keeps the score that needs".

**N2. Line 221: restarts and clause deletion have no citation.** House
rule: every claim about a tool has a source. The preprint behind
`buss2021proof` describes both (a restart "consists of halting the CDCL
search"; its simulation result needs the solver "never to delete
clauses"). Add `[@buss2021proof]` after "as the clause list grows".

**N3. Line 91, Tseitin definition: "for each gate a number of clauses
proportional to its number of inputs".** True of AND and OR. An
$n$-input XOR encoded directly takes $2^n$ clauses, so the size is
linear only once wide XORs are split into two-input ones. Add "for AND
and OR gates" or "for gates of bounded fan-in".

**N4. Line 136: "The first is a chain of the full adder above".**
`gen.ripple` puts a half adder in column 0, which is why the miter has
$45N - 19$ clauses. Line 318 already says so for the BDD. Add "with a
half adder in the first column".

**N5. Line 357: "the inversion of AND 12".** Two sentences earlier
"Line `12 11 9`" uses 12 as a literal (variable 6). Here 12 is the
variable. Say "the inversion of literal 24, variable 12".

**N6. Line 221: "eliminating variables and subsumed clauses
[@een2005preprocessing]".** The entry is "text not consulted"; the title
supports "variable and clause elimination" and not "subsumed". Use the
title's words.

**N7. `@tbl-ch17-engines` is never referenced.** The table at 528 has a
label and no sentence points at it. Add "(@tbl-ch17-engines)" to the
sentence that introduces it, or one before it.

**N8. Exercise 4: "Using only the printed trail".** The levels of 3, 13
and 27 are on the line "levels of its other literals", which is not the
trail. Say "the printed output".

**N9. Line 112: "The size rule that matters survives in every one of
them: clauses grow in proportion to gates."** One five-gate circuit
cannot show proportionality. Say "the rule does not depend on the tool"
and let the next sentence carry it.

**N10. Line 308, In Practice: "a solver has to know how many lie on
each side of every choice".** Stated as necessity, unsourced; counting
is one way to sample uniformly. Soften to "one way to" or cut (see
N16).

already accepts a publisher's Crossref record as the check for some

(b) `bryant1986graph` has the same origin for its given name (research
