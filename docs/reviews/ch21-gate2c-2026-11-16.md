## Non-blocking findings

**N1. Line 175: the reason given for the simulated q99 sitting above the
union bound.** The text says: "The simulated 99th percentile is itself an
estimate from one or four thousand trials, which is why at every size it
sits a few percent above a bound that holds exactly." Sampling error
explains why it *can* sit above. It does not explain why it *does* at every
size: the error is symmetric. For k = 8 the exact q99 is 51 (P(T > 50) =
0.01006, P(T > 51) = 0.00881). In 40 reruns of 4000 trials under other
Python seeds, the simulated q99 came out at 48-53, and at 53 only 3 times.
Python seed 2026 happens to land high at all three sizes, by about 1 to 1.6
standard errors each. A reader could take away that q99 estimates run high.
Fix: "... which is why it can sit above a bound that holds exactly; in this
run it does at every size, by a few percent."

**N2. Line 205 is one paragraph of about 400 words carrying three
arguments.** Each argument is correct, but they run together, and the
numbered "first / second / third" structure is hard to follow in one block.
Fix: split it into three paragraphs at "The second is" and "The third is".

**N3. Line 205: "where reaching `full` alone needs 15".** The spliced line
(`seed-unit/run.out` 47) prints "almost at its measured rate 0.271 needs 15
seeds". This is true of `full` too: same rate, same seeds (line 50). But a
reader matching text to output sees a different bin. Fix: "where reaching
`full` (or `almost`, which goes with it) alone needs 15", or print `full` on
line 47.

**N4. Line 212: "all 28 bins together need 27".** Since round 1 the
chapter's measured answer is 25. The 27 is the union bound, so "need" now
reads as a contradiction of line 196. Fix: "the union bound over all 28 bins
asks for 27".

**N5. Line 606 (summary): "needs no independence between bins and never
under-states".** This holds when the rates are exact. The worked example
(line 559) says that rates from a pilot carry sampling error. Fix: "never
under-states, given the rates".

**N6. Line 455: "By sixteen seeds everything has been seen".** At 16 seeds
the mean seen is 27.9 (`unseen/run.out` 19), and the Chao2 band is
[28.0, 28.5]. Fix: "nearly everything".

**N7. Glossary "Hit-probability vector" (`appG-glossary.qmd` line 258):
"For a fixed generator and seed length".** The chapter's definition (line 39)
is "under this generator, on this design", and it counts run length as part
of the generator. The glossary drops the design, which is the condition the
chapter stresses most (lines 268 and 587). Fix: "For a fixed design and a
fixed generator (its run length included) ...".

**N8. Line 518: "the most it adds is to `pop_x_full`, 0.138 bits".** This
is true. I recomputed I(+n; bin) over all 29 bins from the committed
`info/build/*.dat`: `pop_x_full` 0.138, then `high` 0.106. But only seven
nonzero rows print, and no check covers "the most". Pin it in `info.py`, as
the push_x_full pair is pinned, or say "of the cells printed".

**N9. `refs.bib` `newman1995small` has neither a DOI nor a URL.** The house
rule asks for a locator. The `note` explains why the earlier URL was dropped,
so this is acceptable as a stopgap. Add the DOI when the network allows
checking it.

## Teaching

The chapter reads better than in round 1. The rewritten coupon paragraph now
teaches what makes a union bound tight. Before, the sum over-counted
nothing; now it shows the sum counting multi-bin misses once per bin and
translates the probability gap into seeds. The exact bootstrap law also
removes a seed-dependent number from the chapter's central example. The
clean-seed figure now teaches the correct reading of a 95% bound. The
remaining density is a style matter. N2 is the one place where splitting a
