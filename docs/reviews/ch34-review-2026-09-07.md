## 5. Concept-chapter fitness and originality

**Does each section deliver concept → software practice → verification counterpart?**

- Model (§ch34-model): yes, and the CRV mapping is specific and good (apart from 4.4).
- Prompting: yes; plan-as-instruction-file is specific. The Pitfall callout tying `count == 15` to ch02's implementation-written plan item is the best small move in the chapter.
- Grounding: concept and software practice are full; the verification counterpart is one sentence ("a plan that is a file can be searched"). Missing: which DV sources fit a context and which do not (a waveform does not; a log's relevant line is in the middle, which line 99 uses later; regression history as the thing most worth retrieving). One paragraph would fix it.
- Tools: yes; the simulator-as-tool exposure list and the Makefile parallel are concrete.
- Loop: yes; the coverage-loop parallel is the chapter's central and most original idea and is well made.
- Evaluation: yes, but lines 107–111 read as a benchmark catalogue (HumanEval, SWE-bench, contamination, LiveCodeBench, SWE-ABS, SpecBench, Krakovna, Skalse, SpecBench again) in three paragraphs. The origins are what the author asked for, but the density tips toward survey. Trim to the three that carry the argument (Chen for pass@k, SWE-ABS for the held-out result, Krakovna/Skalse for the name) and move the rest to Further reading.
- Separation: yes; strongest section; the Bergeron extension ("independent of whatever generates the stimulus") is a genuine contribution.
- Multi-agent: concept and failure modes are there; the verification counterpart ("the verification team itself") is generic. Make it specific: name what evidence each human role holds that the others do not (the designer holds intent, the verifier holds the reference model, the formal engineer holds the proof, sign-off holds the plan), and then the agent mapping follows.
- Flow and delegation: yes.

**Tool survey or paper catalogue?** No tools are surveyed; no products are named. The only survey-like passage is 107–111 (above).

**Naive to a DV engineer:** 4.4 (solver/sampler split), 4.11 (a no-overflow property stated on the RTL counter), 4.12 (stimulus writer who may not read the checker), and line 129's "Verification teams enforced the original rule socially, by having a different engineer write the scoreboard": in most teams the same verification engineer writes stimulus and scoreboard; Bergeron's independence is from the *designer*. The chapter's extension is right, but the premise should say so ("independent of the designer; often the same person writes stimulus and checker, which is the gap agents expose").

**Wrong to a software engineer:** line 101, "red, green, refactor, is the loop the coding agents adopted" overgeneralizes; most agent loops are edit-run-fix, and TDD is a discipline imposed on them, which is in fact the chapter's point. Line 69's flat "Practice has moved toward search for code" should acknowledge hybrid retrieval-plus-search is common. Line 57 "the current practice" for instruction files is accurate.

**Originality:** the mappings (prompt = constraint set, plan = instruction file, coverage loop = agent loop, Certitude = mutation testing, gate placement by reversibility, "the boundary moves when a task gains an independent judge") are the chapter's own and are specific. The epigraph earns its place.

## 7. Verdict

**Needs another draft pass.** The chapter's argument and mappings are sound and original, and its sourcing is mostly disciplined, but three findings block author review as it stands.

1. **Publication rule breach (2.1).** The paragraph at line 153 describes AI drafting and reviewing roles, the hook, and the evidence hand-off as the process that writes this book. AGENTS.md forbids any statement of AI assistance in authoring. Delete it.
2. **Figure and alt text contradict the drawings (4.15, 4.16, 4.6).** Four alt texts say left/right where the figures are top/bottom, one says "vertical band" for a horizontal one, the tools caption says "on the right" for a region at the bottom, and the flow prose gives the planner and the debug role inputs the figure does not draw. A reader relying on alt text gets a different diagram from a sighted reader.
3. **Over-claims against the evidence note (1.1, 1.3, 1.4, 1.2).** The summary asserts "search beat retrieval for code" where the note says no head-to-head exists; "since 2007" is a date the Certitude paper does not support; the evaluation figure prints the 90/14 pairing without the caveat the prose gives it; and the TDD-faking "studies" have no citation.

After those, the pass should also cover 4.5 (ch02 has no "gate 1"), 4.11 (the no-overflow property on a four-bit counter, which could become the chapter's best row), 4.13 (reconcile assertion generation as delegable with the checker role as not), 6.1 and 6.2 (callout kind and section order), and the spellings in 6.4–6.5.
