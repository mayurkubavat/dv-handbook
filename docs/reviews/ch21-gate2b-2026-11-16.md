## Blocking findings

### B1. "Simulation agrees with it within its standard error at every size" is false at two of the three sizes (text contradicted by the spliced output)

`chapters/ch21-statistics-random-testing.qmd:175`: "The integral reproduces
$k H_k$ for equal bins, and simulation agrees with it within its standard
error at every size." The block it comments on, `coupon/run.out:3` and
`:6`, prints `E[T] 21.7  sim 22.0 (se 0.1)` and `E[T] 303.6  sim 305.4 (se
1.3)`. A reader sees a gap of three printed standard errors at k = 8 and
1.4 at k = 64. Measured on a scratch copy of `coupon.py` that prints
`(mean - e) / se`: z = 1.45 (k = 8), 1.44 (k = 64), 0.08 (k = 256). The
simulation is fine. The exact E[T] for k = 8 is 21.742857, and the gap is
ordinary noise. But in a chapter about reading such numbers, the
sentence claims a closeness that the output beside it does not show. The
example's own check (`coupon.py:58`) is 4 se, so it does not back the
sentence either. The same paragraph says that "at 64 and 256 bins" the
simulated q99 "can sit a few percent above a bound that holds exactly". It
does so at 8 bins too: sim q99 53 against a union bound of 51. The exact
P(T > 51) is 0.00881, so the bound holds, and 53 is sampling noise.

Fix: "and simulation agrees with it within one and a half standard errors
at every size (the standard error is printed to one decimal, so the k = 8
row looks worse than it is)". Better, print `se` to two decimals at
`coupon.py:53`. Change "at 64 and 256 bins" to "at every size". If the
sentence should be checked, add `C.holds(..., abs(e - mean) <= 2 * se)`.

### B2. The reason given for Chao2's blindness under the 30-cycle generator is wrong (wrong mechanism, contradicted by the definition and by the other generator's run)

`chapters/ch21-statistics-random-testing.qmd:457`: "The estimators are not
unstable here; they are blind. The six missing bins arrive together or not
at all, so no seed has ever shown one of them alone, and there is no
singleton to be the evidence." $Q_1$ counts *bins* seen in exactly one
*seed* (`statlib.py:315`, and the Definition at `:428`). It does not count
seeds that showed one bin alone. If one of the 250 seeds had reached
fifteen entries, it would have hit `almost`, `full` and at least one
`*_x_full` cell. Each of those bins would then have been seen in exactly
one seed, so $Q_1 \ge 3$, $Q_2 = 0$, and Chao2 would have added at least
three bins. Bins that arrive together still make singletons. The
even-odds 200-cycle run shows this happening: at four seeds, $Q_1/Q_2 =
2/2$ on these very bins (`unseen/run.out:17`). In `seed-unit/counts.tsv`,
41 of the 271 seeds that showed `full` hit only one of the four
`op_x_full` cells, so the six do not even arrive together.

The estimators are blind for a simpler reason. No seed reached the
region at all: the chain gives 0.00018 per seed, about 0.04 expected
seeds out of 250 (`unseen/run.out:40-41`). So none of the six bins was
seen even once, and estimators that extrapolate from bins seen once or
twice have nothing to extrapolate from. The chapter's own Pitfall at
`:464` ("rare bins carry little of it") states the right mechanism, so
`:457` contradicts the section it belongs to. The same reasoning appears
in `research/ch21-outline.md` 10.2 ("because the missing bins arrive
together or not at all").

Fix: replace the sentence with: "No seed reached fifteen entries, which
the chain makes the expected outcome (250 seeds times 0.00018 is about
0.04), so none of the six bins was seen even once. Chao2 and Good-Turing
extrapolate from bins seen once or twice, and a region the generator
almost never enters leaves them nothing to extrapolate from." In the same
spirit, soften `:200`. "Hit together or not at all: a seed that fills the
FIFO usually hits all six" is true of 137 of the 271 seeds that show
`full` (51%). Suggested wording: "a seed that never fills the FIFO hits
none of them, and about half of the seeds that do fill it hit all six".

## Non-blocking findings (section N)

### N1. The Verilator check of the DMA generator model cannot tell a wrong model from the right one

`t-way/tway.py:338-358`, chapter `:404-409` ("Does the model of the DMA's
random test match the test? ... Every cell the model gives a chance falls
within 1.52 standard deviations of it"). The check compares per-seed hit
rates of `len_x_out` cells, $1-(1-q)^{20}$, across 50 seeds. Most cells
are hit by nearly every seed, so the test has little power. I swapped
three wrong generators into a scratch copy of `ch11_dist`, and each still
passed the 3.5-sd check, with largest |z| of 1.72, 3.00 and 2.96:

| model in `ch11_dist` | largest per-seed |z| | count-level X2, 9 df, p |
|---|---|---|
| as shipped: length 1:2:4:3, outcome 6:2:2 | 1.52 | 3.1, p = 0.96 |
| outcome 7:1.5:1.5 | 1.72 | 39.9, p = 8e-6 |
| outcome 5:2.5:2.5 | 3.00 | 45.1, p = 9e-7 |
| length 1:3:3:3 | 2.96 | 73.2, p = 4e-12 |

The right-hand column comes from the same 50 `.dat` files. It is a
Pearson test of the summed `len_x_out` counts (1000 transfers) against
$1000\,q$. That test accepts the shipped model and rejects all three
wrong ones. So the conclusion is right, but the evidence printed for it
is weak, in a chapter whose own rule (`:327`) is that a test that does
not reject has not shown that the law holds. Fix: add the count-level
chi-square to section 4 of `tway.py` (about ten lines, using
`S.pearson` over the cells with $q > 0$, plus a check that the zero
cells are zero), check `p > 0.01`, and print it beside the per-seed table.
At `:409`, say that the summed counts fit the model, with the p-value.

### N2. The resampled q99 is a Monte Carlo estimate, and one check compares a theorem with it

`seed-unit/seed_unit.py:172,180,218`, chapter `:116`, `:196`, `:200`,
`:601`. The 2000 shuffled orders estimate the closure law of the empirical
seed set. That law can be computed exactly. Only 11 bins are not hit by
every seed, so inclusion-exclusion needs 2^11 subsets, with
hypergeometric terms for drawing without replacement. My exact
computation gives P(closed by 25) = 0.99077, by 26 = 0.99250 and by 27 =
0.99391. The exact q99 is therefore **25**; the run prints **26** (exact
median 5, as printed). Across Python seeds 2020 to 2039 the estimate
ranged over 24 to 28. With seed 2027 it is 28, and then `C.holds("union
bound is not below the measured q99", u_sum >= q99)` **fails**. That
check tests a theorem (the union bound over empirical rates bounds the
empirical law, with or without replacement) against a noisy estimate,
so a change of seed fails it. The prose's "27 against 26" is still the
right shape (exactly, it is 27 against 25). Fix: compute the curve
exactly (about 15 lines; it also removes the 2000-order loop and its
seed), or keep the resampling and say that 26 is an estimate whose exact
value is 25. Either way, check the bound against the exact curve. The
cycle-model ratio "2.4" (`:121`) would become 2.35.

### N4. Q1/T is printed as a probability

`unseen/run.out:38-39` ("Good-Turing P(next seed adds a bin) = 0.0000"),
`worked/run.out:30-31` ("Good-Turing chance the next seed adds a bin"),
`unseen.py:133-134`, `worked.py:92-94`, `statlib.py:323-326` (docstring:

### E4. Determinism

- Every random source has a fixed seed: Python `random.Random(<const>)`
  in each script, and `+verilator+seed+<s>` per run. There is no
  `time`, `datetime`, `os.environ`, PID or string-hash dependence in any
  script. The `set`s in `tway.py` hold tuples of ints, whose hashes are
  not randomized, and are used only for membership, size and float sums
  printed to 3 or 4 decimals.
- `uniformity/run_draws.sh:22-27`: each of the six processes draws one
  class under the same seed and writes its own file through
  `grep ... > build/<c>.txt`. `uniform.py` reads the files in the fixed
  order of `CLASSES`. The processes write nothing to the shared stderr
  except Verilator's `- ...` report lines, which `check-examples.sh`
  filters out. Output order and content therefore cannot depend on
  scheduling. A failing process aborts the script through `wait "$p"`
  under `set -e` and `pipefail`, and a short file fails the pinned
  `draws per class`.
- Verilator's walltime lines start with `- ` and never reach a `run.out`.
- The draws depend on the z3 release. See N7.
