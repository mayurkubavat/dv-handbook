# Research note: Verification Planning

Scope: what a verification plan is according to canonical and standards sources, how features are extracted from a specification and traced to tests and coverage, how the coverage model is derived from the plan, what tools and formats exist (with OpenTitan as the open primary example), sign-off criteria, known failure modes with survey evidence, and the state of AI-assisted planning. Labels used throughout: **[methodology]** for textbook or cookbook guidance, **[standard]** for normative documents, **[primary/open]** for open-source project artefacts, **[vendor]** for marketing or product documentation, **[survey]** for industry studies, **[research]** for peer-reviewed or preprint results, **[industry paper]** for conference papers by practitioners.

Note on method: the session's web-search quota was exhausted at the start of this run, so sources were reached by direct URL fetch of known pages, GitHub raw files, and PDF downloads. Gaps are flagged in section 9 rather than filled by guesswork.

## 1. What a verification plan is (canonical sources)

### 1.1 Textbooks

- **Bergeron, *Writing Testbenches* (2003)** [methodology], existing key `bergeron2003testbenches`. Bergeron's "Verification Plan" chapter frames the plan as the document that answers, before any testbench is written, *what* will be verified, by *which* method (simulation, formal, emulation), at *which* level (unit, block, system), and *when the job is done*. He argues for deriving features from the specification rather than from the implementation, ordering them by priority, and separating "must-have" from "nice-to-have" features for first silicon. *Confidence: moderate. The Springer publisher page could not be fetched in this run (login redirect), so the chapter structure is from prior knowledge of the book; verify page numbers before quoting.* Publisher DOI: [10.1007/978-1-4615-0302-6](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-1-4615-0302-6).
- **Wile, Goss, Roesner, *Comprehensive Functional Verification* (2005)** [methodology], existing key `wile2005cfv`. The book's "verification cycle" puts the verification plan as the first artefact after the specification and treats it as a joint product of design, verification and architecture. Its plan outline covers: functions to be verified per hierarchy level, the environment per level, the coverage requirements, and the completion criteria. *Confidence: moderate; the ScienceDirect page was reachable but its table of contents is loaded dynamically and could not be extracted. Verify chapter numbers before quoting.* Publisher: [ScienceDirect, ISBN 9780127518039](https://www.sciencedirect.com/book/9780127518039/comprehensive-functional-verification).
- **Piziali, *Functional Verification Coverage Measurement and Analysis* (2004)** [methodology], new key `piziali2004coverage`. The standard reference on deriving a *coverage model* from a plan: feature identification, attribute and value selection, top-down coverage model design, and the split between implicit (code) and explicit (functional) coverage. Publisher DOI: [10.1007/b117979](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/b117979). *Same fetch limitation as Bergeron.*

### 1.2 Industry-paper definition (DVCon)

Ehlers, Vargas (Freescale) and Carzola (Cadence), "Best Practices in Verification Planning", DVCon US 2013 [industry paper], new key `ehlers2013planning`. Abstract: "Creating meaningful verification plans is an art form yet to be fully codified. Failure to plan means planning to fail. The development of verification plans is the most crucial step in the functional verification process. Oddly, not every company takes the time to establish an in-house prescriptive and repeatable approach." The paper describes a "complete verification flow including verification strategy, planning, change management and closure", recommends that "the template for the verification plan should be created directly within the target documentation/planning tool", and warns that "frequent design change requests and late design changes ... can often invalidate a large portion of work already done in the verification planning process" [DVCon Proceedings, 2013](https://dvcon-proceedings.org/document/best-practices-in-verification-planning/), PDF: https://dvcon-proceedings.org/wp-content/uploads/best-practices-in-verification-planning.pdf.

### 1.3 Verification Academy / Coverage Cookbook

The Siemens Verification Academy Coverage Cookbook (existing key `va-coverage-cookbook`) [methodology] is the most widely used free planning text. Its planning chapters are "Specification to Test Plan", "Executable Test Plan Format", and "Testplan to Functional Coverage", all attributed to Harry Foster and last updated December 2013. The public preview of "Specification to Test Plan" states the aim: "The goal in creating a coverage model spreadsheet or test plan is to capture a subset of the design intent and behavior that is targeted for functional coverage" [Verification Academy, Specification to Test Plan](https://verificationacademy.com/cookbook/coverage/specification-to-testplan). The "Testplan to Functional Coverage" preview lists the four questions the plan must answer: "What features need to be tested", "Under what conditions the features need to be tested", "What testbench infrastructure is required to drive and monitor the design's interfaces", and "How the testbench will check that the features work" [Verification Academy, Test Plan to Functional Coverage](https://verificationacademy.com/cookbook/coverage/testplan-to-functional-coverage). The "Requirements Writing Guidelines" page says requirements "need to be recorded in a useful, easy to digest manner" before planning starts and be classed as rules (mandatory) or suggestions [Verification Academy, Requirements Writing Guidelines](https://verificationacademy.com/cookbook/coverage/requirements-writing-guidelines). *The full body of these three pages is behind a free login; the spreadsheet column list (Section, Title, Description, Link, Type, Weight, Goal) that the book may want to reproduce was not verifiable in this run and should be confirmed by logging in.*

### 1.4 IEEE 1012 (V&V plan)

IEEE Std 1012-2024, "IEEE Standard for System, Software, and Hardware Verification and Validation", existing key `ieee1012-2024` [standard], was board-approved 2024-11-12 and published 2025-08-22. It defines V&V as "analysis, evaluation, review, inspection, assessment, and testing of products" and specifies "V&V life cycle process requirements ... for different integrity levels" [IEEE SA](https://standards.ieee.org/ieee/1012/7324/). Relevance: it is the only IEEE standard that prescribes the *contents* of a V&V plan (VVP); it is written for systems and software, and industry DV plans are looser than its outline. *The VVP clause text itself was not accessible; do not quote clause numbers without checking the standard.*

### 1.5 UCIS 1.0 (coverage interchange, not plan format)

Accellera's Unified Coverage Interoperability Standard Version 1.0 is dated 2012-06-02 [standard], new key `ucis2012`. Accellera's page calls it "a first step towards the creation of an application interface (API) that allows for interoperability of verification coverage data across multiple tools from multiple vendors" [Accellera, UCIS](https://www.accellera.org/downloads/standards/ucis). The standard's glossary does define a plan object: "verification plan hierarchy (or test plan hierarchy) – A part of the UCIS data model whose nodes are linked to coverage or instance or design unit data structures for purposes of analyzing coverage in context of a verification plan", with "tag – A name associated with a scope; characteristically used to link verification plan scopes with instance, coverage, or design unit scopes". Each run is a "test record" (`UCIS_HISTORYNODE_TEST`), "the history node with which coveritem counts may be associated", and merged databases add a merge history node [UCIS 1.0 PDF, 2012-06-02](https://www.accellera.org/images/downloads/standards/ucis/UCIS_Version_1.0_Final_June-2012.pdf). So UCIS gives a plan-tracking tool a portable way to attach coverage results to plan nodes and to trace which test produced them, but it does not standardise how a plan is authored.

## 2. Feature extraction from a specification

### 2.1 Methodology guidance

- The Coverage Cookbook's four questions (section 1.3) are the standard decomposition: feature, conditions, infrastructure, check. Its requirements-writing page insists requirements be compiled and classified (rule vs suggestion) *before* planning [Verification Academy](https://verificationacademy.com/cookbook/coverage/requirements-writing-guidelines).
- Ehlers et al. (2013) identify the worst case as "an undefined design component or design feature which has no documented description and requires a significant amount of research and effort on the part of the verification engineer to develop a meaningful verification plan" [DVCon 2013](https://dvcon-proceedings.org/document/best-practices-in-verification-planning/). The chapter can use this to motivate spec review as part of planning.
- OpenTitan's methodology makes the same point from the review side: "The feedback in this review flows both ways - the language in the design specification could be made more precise, and missing items in both the design specification and the testplan can be identified and added" [OpenTitan DV methodology](https://opentitan.org/book/doc/contributing/dv/methodology/index.html).
- OpenTitan's testpoint naming convention is a de facto decomposition rule: a name is "a single lower_snake_case string that succinctly describes the intended feature" using `<main_feature>_<sub_feature>_<sub_sub_feature_or_type>_...` [dvsim testplanner doc](https://github.com/lowRISC/dvsim/blob/master/doc/testplanner.md). Shared plans for register, interrupt, alert, and bus-access features are imported rather than rewritten (see section 4.2), which is a concrete instance of "interface / register / mode" decomposition.

### 2.2 Traceability chain

The chain requirement → feature → test → coverage point → result appears explicitly in Kreisinger and Chatterjee (Allegro Microsystems), "Verification Plan in Requirements Management Tool: Simple Traceability and Automated Interface to Regression Manager", DVCon US 2024 [industry paper], new key `kreisinger2024traceability`: "Requirements shall be traceable through the verification plan to regression results, which shall be demonstrable and visible to all members of the development team." Their flow keeps the plan inside Jama Connect alongside the requirements, links plan elements to Verisium Manager regression results, and uses the tool's *suspect link* mechanism for change propagation: "In case any of the upstream items have been changed, all downstream relationships become suspect ... Having a verification plan clear of suspect relationships is a necessary condition for verification closure." They also note that "system requirements should be traceable to the regression results to fulfill part of the functional safety standards such as ISO 26262" [DVCon 2024, PDF](https://dvcon-proceedings.org/wp-content/uploads/1009.pdf).

### 2.3 What ISO 26262 and DO-254 require (public summaries)

- **ISO 26262:2018** [standard; summarised from Wikipedia, not the standard text], new key `iso26262-2018`. "Road vehicles – Functional safety", first published 2011, revised 2018 in twelve parts; Part 5 covers hardware development, Part 8 "supporting processes" covers configuration and change management, verification planning and tool qualification, and Part 11 is "Guidelines on application of ISO 26262 to semiconductors". ASIL A–D are derived from severity, exposure and controllability. The standard emphasises "traceability between dependent work products and identification of all changes" [Wikipedia, ISO 26262](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISO_26262). Safe wording for the chapter: ISO 26262 requires a documented verification plan and bidirectional traceability between safety requirements and verification evidence, with rigour scaled by ASIL; cite the standard itself for clause-level claims.
- **RTCA DO-254 (2000)** [standard; summarised from Wikipedia], new key `do254-2000`. "Design Assurance Guidance for Airborne Electronic Hardware", RTCA/EUROCAE, 2000, recognised by FAA AC 20-152 in 2005 as "an acceptable means of compliance". Five Design Assurance Levels A–E. It mandates "capturing and tracking of requirements throughout the design and verification process"; verification engineers "generate test plans proving implementation compliance", and the key documents are the Plan for Hardware Aspects of Certification (PHAC), Hardware Verification Plan (HVP) and Hardware Accomplishment Summary (HAS) [Wikipedia, DO-254](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DO-254). Safe wording: DO-254 is requirements-based; every requirement must trace to a verification activity and result, and for Levels A/B elemental analysis (typically code coverage) is additionally expected.

## 3. Coverage model design from the plan

- **Two-part plan.** OpenTitan states the plan "consist[s] of two parts: A testplan that captures at a high level, a list of tests that are being planned to verify all design features listed in the design specification" and "A functional coverage plan that captures at high level a list of functional coverage points and coverage crosses needed to verify that the features listed in the design specification is tested by the list of tests" [OpenTitan DV methodology README](https://github.com/lowRISC/opentitan/blob/master/doc/contributing/dv/methodology/README.md). This is the cleanest open statement that tests and coverage are planned separately but from the same feature list.
- **Covergroup entries in the plan.** In the OpenTitan schema each planned covergroup has a `name` (lower_snake_case, suffixed `_cg`) and a `desc` describing which coverpoints and crosses it should contain, for example: "Cover various combinations of rv_timer inputs when generating a timeout. Cover appropriate ranges of values of step and prescaler as coverpoint buckets and cross them with each other" [dvsim testplanner doc](https://github.com/lowRISC/dvsim/blob/master/doc/testplanner.md). The HMAC plan has 12 such covergroups covering configuration, status bits, error codes, message length, register writes during hashing, digest reads and save/restore [hmac_testplan.hjson](https://github.com/lowRISC/opentitan/blob/master/hw/ip/hmac/data/hmac_testplan.hjson).
- **Code vs functional coverage.** OpenTitan collects line, toggle, FSM (state and transition), conditional and branch coverage plus assertion coverage, and defines functional coverage as explicit, manually created points capturing "whether signals (that reflect the current state of the design) have met an interesting set or a sequence of values". Its collection policy: toggle on DUT and sub-module IOs only; all metrics except toggle on non-pre-verified sub-modules; treat pre-verified sub-modules as black boxes [OpenTitan DV methodology](https://opentitan.org/book/doc/contributing/dv/methodology/index.html).
- **Holes, exclusions and waivers.** OpenTitan requires every exclusion to carry an annotation prefix: `UNR` (unreachable), `NON_RTL` (simulation-only constructs), `UNSUPPORTED` (feature not in the product spec), `EXTERNAL` (covered in another testbench), `LOW_RISK` (prohibitively difficult, low-risk corner cases); designers must sign off exclusions in PR review [OpenTitan DV methodology](https://opentitan.org/book/doc/contributing/dv/methodology/index.html). For the V2S security stage the checklist adds that countermeasure blocks previously excluded must have "these exclusions ... removed" [OpenTitan checklist](https://opentitan.org/book/doc/project_governance/checklist/index.html).
- **Goals.** OpenTitan's goals are stage-based: 90 percent code and functional coverage at V2, 100 percent "with waivers" at V3 (section 5). The Coverage Cookbook's executable-testplan format carries per-item weight and goal columns, but that page could not be read in this run (section 9).

## 4. Tools and formats

### 4.1 Spreadsheets and vendor planners [vendor]

- **Spreadsheets** remain the baseline; Ehlers et al. explicitly allow "if the verification plan tool to be used is a spreadsheet, the verification plan template should be created as a spreadsheet" [DVCon 2013](https://dvcon-proceedings.org/document/best-practices-in-verification-planning/). The Coverage Cookbook's "executable test plan" is a spreadsheet or XML document read by the coverage tool.
- **Cadence Verisium Manager (formerly vManager), vPlan.** The Cadence product page is behind a bot-check and could not be fetched. The only fetched description is in Kreisinger 2024, which uses "Cadence Verisium Manager [2] verification platform ... to run and analyze regression" and notes that commercial Jama-to-Verisium integrations "require a Verisium Manager full client license to create and edit the verification plan" [DVCon 2024](https://dvcon-proceedings.org/wp-content/uploads/1009.pdf). Product URL for the bibliography: https://www.cadence.com/en_US/home/tools/system-design-and-verification/ai-driven-verification/verisium-manager.html (not verified this run). New key `cadence-verisium-manager`.
- **Synopsys Verdi VMS / Planner.** Synopsys states Verdi "empowers you to plan, execute and determine coverage of your simulation regressions" and that "Verdi VMS (Verification Managment System) includes planning, managing compilation, regression test execution, data collection, reporting and tracking of the design verification process. Automates coverage-driven verification flows, tracks and collects regression results data in a relational database, and supports annotation of coverage results" [Synopsys, Verdi](https://www.synopsys.com/verification/debug/verdi.html); the VMS page adds "Plan, execute & aggregate functional coverage of regressions" [Synopsys, Verdi VMS](https://www.synopsys.com/verification/debug/verdi-vms.html). The older "VC Execution Manager" URL now returns 404. New key `synopsys-verdi-vms`.
- **Siemens Questa One Verification IQ, Testplan Author.** Siemens describes "Testplan Author" as optimising "verification traceability with team-based verification plan creation for plan and requirements-driven processes. Providing traceability from requirements to verification results and design implementation", and frames methodology as having "evolved from coverage-driven to plan-and-requirements-driven and now to data-driven" [Siemens EDA, Questa verification management](https://eda.sw.siemens.com/en-US/ic/questa/simulation/verification-management/). New key `siemens-questa-vm`.

### 4.2 OpenTitan: the open primary source [primary/open]

OpenTitan (lowRISC) is the strongest open primary source for a machine-readable plan format because the schema, the tool, dozens of real plans, and the sign-off criteria are all public and in use.

- **Format.** "The testplan is written in Hjson format ... human-writable and machine-parseable, which facilitates an automated and documentation-driven DV effort." The `testplanner` tool parses it to "insert the testplan and coverage plan as tables into the DV document itself, so that all of the required information is in one place" and to "annotate the nightly regression results to allow us to track our progress towards executing the testplan and coverage collection" [OpenTitan DV methodology](https://opentitan.org/book/doc/contributing/dv/methodology/index.html). New key `opentitan-dv-methodology`.
- **Schema** (from the dvsim testplanner doc, new key `opentitan-testplanner`): top-level `name`, optional `import_testplans` (paths relative to `$REPO_TOP`, with `{name}` and `{intf}` wildcards expanded per block and interface), a `testpoints` list and a `covergroups` list. Each testpoint has `name` (lower_snake_case feature path), `stage` ("either V1, V2, V2S or V3"), `desc` (Markdown; the recommended template is "**Goal** / **Stimulus** / **Check**"), `tests` ("initially empty - it is gradually updated as tests are written"), and optional `tags` such as `["verilator", "fpga_cw310"]` or `["gls", "pa"]`. Each covergroup has `name` (suffixed `_cg`) and `desc` [dvsim testplanner.md](https://github.com/lowRISC/dvsim/blob/master/doc/testplanner.md). *Note: the original task brief listed a `milestone` field; the current schema calls it `stage`. Older OpenTitan plans used `milestone`, so the book should say "stage (historically milestone)".*
- **Worked example: HMAC.** `hw/ip/hmac/data/hmac_testplan.hjson` imports the shared CSR, alert, interrupt, stress-with-reset, TL device-access-type and HMAC security-countermeasure plans, then lists block-specific testpoints such as `smoke` (V1, tests `["hmac_smoke"]`), `long_msg` (V2, "The message length is between 0 and 10,000 bytes"), and `back_pressure` (V2, "disabled all the protocol delays to make sure to have high chance of hitting the FIFO_FULL status"), followed by 12 covergroups [hmac_testplan.hjson](https://github.com/lowRISC/opentitan/blob/master/hw/ip/hmac/data/hmac_testplan.hjson). The shared CSR plan shows wildcard test names such as `"{name}{intf}_csr_hw_reset"` and mandates that each CSR test "run ... for all available interfaces the CSRs are accessible from" [csr_testplan.hjson](https://github.com/lowRISC/opentitan/blob/master/hw/dv/tools/dvsim/testplans/csr_testplan.hjson).
- **Counter-example: UART.** `hw/ip/uart/data/uart_testplan.hjson` has 20 testpoints but its covergroups section is a single placeholder `foo_cg` with an empty description [uart_testplan.hjson](https://github.com/lowRISC/opentitan/blob/master/hw/ip/uart/data/uart_testplan.hjson). This is useful primary evidence for the chapter's "plan stubs that never get filled" failure mode, and shows that even a disciplined open project has uneven plan quality across blocks.
- **Living plan.** The mechanism is: tests are named in the plan's `tests` field; the nightly regression results are keyed by test name; `testplanner` joins the two to produce a per-testpoint table of passing/total counts and a per-stage progress figure; the DV document is regenerated from the plan and links to the latest regression and coverage dashboards [OpenTitan DV methodology](https://opentitan.org/book/doc/contributing/dv/methodology/index.html). The plan is therefore the source of truth for progress reporting, and "SIM_ALL_TESTS_PASSING" in the checklist is defined against it.

## 5. Sign-off criteria and metrics

### 5.1 OpenTitan stages and checklist [primary/open]

OpenTitan's development stages page defines verification stages (new key `opentitan-dev-stages`): V0 "Testbench being developed, not functional; testplan being written; decided which methodology to use (sim-based DV, FPV, or both)"; V1 environment operational with documentation and smoke tests; V2 "all interfaces hooked up and exercised, all assertions written and enabled" with "90% code coverage across the board and 90% functional coverage"; V2S "all tests for security countermeasures"; V3 "all bugs addressed, all tests including newly added post-V2 tests (if any), all tests with all seeds passing, 100% code and 100% functional coverage with waivers". V2 aligns with design stage D2 and V3 with D3 [OpenTitan development stages](https://opentitan.org/book/doc/project_governance/development_stages.html).

The sign-off checklist (new key `opentitan-checklist`) turns these into named items [OpenTitan checklist](https://opentitan.org/book/doc/project_governance/checklist/index.html):

| Stage | Selected items (verbatim identifiers) |
|---|---|
| V1 | `DV_DOC_DRAFT_COMPLETED`, `TESTPLAN_COMPLETED` (Hjson plan with testpoints mapped to features plus functional coverage plan), `SIM_SMOKE_TEST_PASSING`, `SIM_CSR_MEM_TEST_SUITE_PASSING`, `SIM_NIGHTLY_REGRESSION_SETUP` (recommended 100 seeds), `SIM_COVERAGE_MODEL_ADDED`, `DESIGN_SPEC_REVIEWED`, `TESTPLAN_REVIEWED` |
| V2 | `DESIGN_DELTAS_CAPTURED_V2`, `FUNCTIONAL_COVERAGE_IMPLEMENTED`, `ALL_ASSERTION_CHECKS_ADDED`, `SIM_ALL_TESTS_PASSING` ("written and are passing with at least one random seed"), `SIM_NIGHTLY_REGRESSION_V2` ("90% passing"), `SIM_CODE_COVERAGE_V2` (line, toggle, fsm, branch, assertion at 90%), `SIM_FUNCTIONAL_COVERAGE_V2` (90%), `FPV_COI_COVERAGE_V2` (75%), `NO_HIGH_PRIORITY_ISSUES_PENDING`, `ALL_LOW_PRIORITY_ISSUES_ROOT_CAUSED`, `DV_DOC_TESTPLAN_REVIEWED` |
| V2S | `SEC_CM_TESTPLAN_COMPLETED`, `FPV_SEC_CM_PROVEN`, `SIM_SEC_CM_VERIFIED`, `SIM_COVERAGE_REVIEWED` |
| V3 | `DESIGN_DELTAS_CAPTURED_V3`, `X_PROP_ANALYSIS_COMPLETED`, `SIM_NIGHTLY_REGRESSION_AT_V3` ("100% passing (with 1 week minimum soak time)"), `SIM_CODE_COVERAGE_AT_100`, `SIM_FUNCTIONAL_COVERAGE_AT_100`, `FPV_ASSERTIONS_PROVEN_AT_V3`, `ALL_TODOS_RESOLVED`, `NO_TOOL_WARNINGS_THROWN`, `TB_LINT_COMPLETE`, `NO_ISSUES_PENDING` |

Two further OpenTitan statements matter for the chapter. On regression: "Achieving 100% pass rate in our nightly regressions consistently is a key to asserting 'verification complete'", with each constrained-random test run with "a sufficiently large number of seeds (arbitrarily chosen to be 100)". On review: "The DV document and the completed testplan documents are reviewed face-to-face" with designers, DV peers, leads and integration teams, intermediate reviews happen in GitHub PRs against the checklist, and a final sign-off review confirms all items are done without major exceptions [OpenTitan DV methodology README](https://github.com/lowRISC/opentitan/blob/master/doc/contributing/dv/methodology/README.md).

### 5.2 Methodology sources on closure

- Kreisinger 2024: a plan "clear of suspect relationships is a necessary condition for verification closure", i.e. every upstream requirement change must have been re-analysed [DVCon 2024](https://dvcon-proceedings.org/wp-content/uploads/1009.pdf).
- Ehlers 2013 lists closure as the last of four phases (strategy, planning, change management, closure) and notes that ad hoc planning gives "a lower sense as to the certainty of closure and completion" [DVCon 2013](https://dvcon-proceedings.org/document/best-practices-in-verification-planning/).
- Bug-rate convergence as a sign-off input is a common practice claim (Bergeron, Wile) but no survey or open-project source with numbers was reached in this run; see section 8.

## 6. Failure modes and evidence

- **Plans written late or not at all.** Ehlers 2013: "Some verification engineers would like to treat verification planning as an afterthought if it is done at all. This often leads to an ad hoc approach to the planning process, little to no consistency among verification documentation" [DVCon 2013](https://dvcon-proceedings.org/document/best-practices-in-verification-planning/).
- **Plans not updated after design change.** Ehlers 2013 on late changes invalidating planning work (section 1.2); Kreisinger 2024's suspect-link mechanism exists precisely because spec changes otherwise fail to propagate [DVCon 2024](https://dvcon-proceedings.org/wp-content/uploads/1009.pdf). OpenTitan's `DESIGN_DELTAS_CAPTURED_V2/V3` checklist items are the open-project countermeasure [OpenTitan checklist](https://opentitan.org/book/doc/project_governance/checklist/index.html).
- **Placeholder coverage plans.** The OpenTitan UART plan's `foo_cg` stub (section 4.2) is direct evidence.
- **Survey evidence on specifications as a bug cause** [survey]. Harry Foster's write-up of the 2022 Wilson Research Group study (existing key `foster2022wrg`) says: "The data suggest design errors are the leading cause of functional flaws, and the situation is worsening. In addition, problems associated with changing, incorrect, and incomplete specifications are a common theme often voiced by many verification engineers and project managers" (Fig. 12-5, "Root Cause of Functional Flaws"). The same post reports that about 24 percent of IC/ASIC projects achieved first-silicon success in 2022 and 66 percent were behind schedule [Siemens Verification Horizons, Part 12, 2023-01-09](https://blogs.sw.siemens.com/verificationhorizons/2023/01/09/part-12-the-2022-wilson-research-group-functional-verification-study/). *The per-category percentages for specification-related root causes are in the figure image, not the text; quote them only after reading the report PDF (existing keys `foster2020wrg`, `foster2022wrg`, `foster2024wrg`).*
- **Survey evidence on where time goes** [survey]. The 2022 study's Part 8 reports that "IC/ASIC verification engineers spend more of their time debugging than with any other activity" (Fig. 8-4, which also has test planning, testbench development, and test creation/running as categories), and that verification-engineer demand grew at a 6.2 percent CAGR from 2007 to 2022 against 2.7 percent for design engineers [Siemens Verification Horizons, Part 8, 2022-12-12](https://blogs.sw.siemens.com/verificationhorizons/2022/12/12/part-8-the-2022-wilson-research-group-functional-verification-study/). The 2024 study reports first-silicon success for "only 14% of ASIC/SoC projects", "the lowest figure in more than twenty years", and that "60–70% of engineering effort in chip projects belongs in verification" [Foster, Siemens blog, 2025-09-03](https://blogs.sw.siemens.com/verificationhorizons/2025/09/03/why-first-silicon-success-is-getting-harder-for-system-companies/); report landing page: [Siemens, 2024 WRG IC/ASIC report](https://resources.sw.siemens.com/en-US/white-paper-2024-wilson-research-group-ic-asic-functional-verification-trend-report/). *The exact time-split percentages (planning vs testbench vs tests vs debug) were not readable from the fetched text; they are in figures. Do not quote numbers for them without the report PDF.*

## 7. AI-assisted planning

- **Research.** Kochar, Pinckney, Liu, Ho, Deng, Ren, Khailany (NVIDIA), "GRPO with State Mutations: Improving LLM-Based Hardware Test Plan Generation", arXiv 2601.07593, submitted 2026-01-12 [research], new key `kochar2026grpo`. The paper separates test-plan generation from testbench execution and reports that DeepSeek-R1 and Claude 4 Sonnet achieve "only 15.7-21.7% success rates" while their fine-tuned 7B model reaches "a 33.3% golden test pass rate and a 13.9% mutation detection rate", "a 17.6% absolute improvement over baseline" [arXiv, 2026-01-12](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.07593). Takeaway for the chapter: LLM-written plans pass a golden design a third of the time at best and detect mutated (buggy) designs far less often, so plan review remains a human job.
- **Industry paper (formal).** Jain, Rawat, Prajapati (Intel), "GenAI Leap in Formal Verification Testplanning", DVCon India 2024 [industry paper], new key `jain2024genai-fv-testplan`: prompt-engineered multimodal LLMs produced formal test plans with "a minimum of 80% accuracy" on three designs of varying complexity [DVCon India 2024, PDF](https://dvcon-proceedings.org/wp-content/uploads/2B3-DVCon_India_2024_Final_Paper_1423.pdf). *Accuracy is self-assessed by the authors against their own reference plans; treat as a pilot.*

## 8. Commonly repeated but unsourced claims

| Claim | What sources say | Safe wording |
|---|---|---|
| "The verification plan is the most important document in the project." | Ehlers 2013 says planning is "the most crucial step" (opinion in an industry paper); Bergeron and Wile argue it at length but do not quantify. | "Practitioners consistently rank planning as the most important step (Ehlers et al. 2013; Bergeron 2003)." |

| "Coverage closure means 100 percent functional coverage." | OpenTitan requires 100 percent "with waivers" at V3 and 90 percent at V2; waivers are annotated and designer-approved. | "Closure is 100 percent of the *planned* coverage after documented, reviewed waivers." |
